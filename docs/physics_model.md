# The physics model

How the simulation turns tool motion into forces, and which numbers are measured, guessed or
chosen for numerical reasons. File and constant names refer to `scenes/`.

## Units and frame

- **SI everywhere:** metres, kilograms, seconds, so forces are in newtons and moduli in pascals.
  SOFA itself has no units; mixing mm with N/m would silently give wrong forces.
- **Frame (rig scenes 03–06, `rig.py`):** origin at the centre of the sponge's bottom face; x along
  the 86 mm side (right in the overhead camera view), y along the 71 mm side (up in that view),
  z up. The undeformed top surface is at z = 21 mm (`rig_tools.TOP`).
- **Time step:** `DT = 0.01` s (100 Hz) in every scene.

## The sponge (`rig.py`)

| Quantity | Value | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Size | 86 × 71 × 21 mm (`SPONGE_SIZE`) | measured from photos |
| Layers | 15 mm cream foam base (`BASE_THICKNESS`) + 6 mm orange scrub layer on top | measured from photos |
| Young's modulus | 30 kPa base (`YOUNG_BASE`), 60 kPa scrub (`YOUNG_SCRUB`) | **guesses, to calibrate** |
| Poisson's ratio | 0.3 (`POISSON`) | guess |
| Density | 30 kg/m³ (`DENSITY`) → 3.85 g sponge, 0.038 N weight | guess |

**Mesh.** A regular grid of 19 × 16 × 8 nodes (`RES`, ~4.8 × 4.7 × 3 mm spacing), each hexahedral
cell split into 6 tetrahedra: 2432 nodes, 11 340 tetrahedra (`block_on_supports.make_mesh`).
Tetrahedra rather than hexahedra so the mesh can be split for cutting. The layer interface
(z = 15 mm) falls on a node plane, and each tetrahedron gets the modulus of the layer its centroid
is in.

**Material law.** Corotational linear elasticity (`TetrahedronFEMForceField`, `method="large"`):
each tetrahedron's rotation is removed before Hooke's law is applied, so large rotations are handled
while the stress–strain law stays linear. Good for moderate strains (indentations of a few mm);
foam is in reality non-linear and viscoelastic, which is not modelled yet.

**Dynamics.** Implicit Euler (`EulerImplicitSolver`) with a little mass damping
(`rayleighMass = 0.1`), and **no** stiffness damping: stiffness damping acts against absolute
velocity, and while stiff springs moved it absorbed force that never reached the load cells.
Mass from `MeshMatrixMass`. The linear system is solved with a direct sparse LDLᵀ factorisation
(`SparseLDLSolver`), refactorised every step.

## The load cells (`rig.py`, `rig_tools.add_cell_plates`)

Eight straight bar load cells, two per side of the sponge, at ±14 mm from each side's midline
(`PAIR_OFFSET`). Each bar is 12.7 mm wide and reaches 12 mm under the sponge edge
(`BAR_OVERLAP`). The sponge is **glued** to the bar tips (as on the real rig), so cells can read
negative values (tension) when the sponge tips or is lifted.

Channels, numbered clockwise from the top-left in the overhead view (`rig.CELLS`):

```
            CH1        CH2              y
        +---[]---------[]---+           ^
     CH8[]                  []CH3       |
        |      sponge       |           +--> x
     CH7[]                  []CH4
        +---[]---------[]---+
            CH6        CH5
```

This numbering is ours; the real amplifier channel order has not been mapped yet.

**Cell model `"plate"` (default, `rig.CELL_MODEL`).** Each bar tip is a rigid plate (1 g) on a
6-DOF spring to its rest pose: vertical/linear stiffness `CELL_K = 3e4` N/m (1 N compresses it
33 µm) and tilt stiffness `CELL_KA = 50` N·m/rad (the real double-bending-beam cells keep their tip
level). The sponge's bottom nodes over the tip are welded to the plate by bilateral constraints,
so the contact patch moves as one piece like the metal tip. **Reading = the vertical plate spring
force** (plus the plate's own weight, so it reads the force from the sponge only). Positive =
sponge pushes down on the cell.

**Cell model `"springs"`** (set `rig.CELL_MODEL = "springs"`): an independent vertical spring of
`CELL_K / n` on each of the n nodes over the tip. Over a 2 s test the two models agree within
~6 mN per cell; `"springs"` is about 2.2× faster.

Scenes 01–03 use simpler spring cells of their own (four square supports in 01–02; per-node springs
in 03).

## The instruments (`tools.py`, `rig_tools.py`)

- **Grasper:** the Large Needle Driver (EndoWrist 400006) jaws from the
  [dVRK model](https://github.com/jhu-dvrk/dvrk_model): two serrated 9.65 mm jaws, opening about
  the jaw pivot.
- **Knife ("blade" in the code and data):** a #11 scalpel blade in a cap on the EndoWrist wrist,
  generated by `scripts/make_scalpel.py`.

Each part (`jaw_left`, `jaw_right`, `blade`) has a ~6000-triangle render mesh and a ~400-triangle
collision mesh in `assets/`.

**Position control with a force sensor.** Each part is a rigid body (3 g) tied to a
**kinematic target** by a stiff 6-DOF spring: `SENSOR_K = 1e6` N/m (1 µm per N) and
`SENSOR_KA = 50` N·m/rad. The script moves the target along the commanded path; the body follows
and collides with the sponge. The spring is the part's **force/torque sensor**:

```
force on sponge  = SENSOR_K * (target_position - body_position) + part_mass * g
torque on sponge = SENSOR_KA * rotation_vector(R_target * R_body^T)      (about the part's pivot)
```

These are logged as `<part>_force`, `<part>_torque` and `<part>_pose` (pivot position +
quaternion x, y, z, w). `scripts/wrench.py` combines the parts into one 6-axis wrench per instrument
(grasper = both jaws + grasp springs; scalpel), in the world frame and in the tool frame.

**Contact.** Lagrange-multiplier contact with Coulomb friction
(`FreeMotionAnimationLoop` + `BlockGaussSeidelConstraintSolver`, tolerance 1e-8, up to 500
iterations). Proximity detection with `LocalMinDistance` (alarm distance 2 mm, contact distance
0.2 mm) between the parts' collision meshes and the sponge's surface triangles, edges and points.
Friction `mu = 1.0` between tools and foam in scenes 04–06 (`rig_tools.FRICTION`), 0.3 for the
sphere in 02–03.

**Bent wrist (render only).** The jaws and the scalpel are simulated in their commanded poses. For
the videos, `tools.wrist` solves the EndoWrist roll/pitch/yaw so that the shaft runs towards the
arm at ~45° (`rig.SHAFT_DIRS`). The shaft and clevis are drawn only; they do not collide.

## Grasping (`rig_tools.Recorder.update_grasp`)

Friction alone on this coarse mesh cannot hold a pinch: the foam slips out as the jaws rise. So, as
in most SOFA surgical simulators, a closed grasp is an **attachment**: at `T_GRASP` the sponge nodes
between the closed jaw tips are tied by springs (`GRASP_K = 2e3` N/m each, ramped in over 0.2 s) to
anchors that move rigidly with the grasper, until `T_RELEASE`. Those springs count towards the
grasper's force (`grasp_force` in the data).

## Cutting (`cutting.py`, `rig.split_mesh`)

Zero-width cutting by **releasing ties**:

1. When the mesh is built, it is **split along a node plane** (x = 22 mm in scene 05; the layer
   interface z = 15 mm in scene 06). Every node on the plane gets a twin; the tetrahedra on one
   side use the twins.
2. Each node/twin pair is held together by a stiff zero-length spring (`TIE_K = 2e4` N/m, ~70× an
   element's stiffness), so the uncut plane behaves like uncut foam.
3. **Cutting rule**, every step: if the blade pushes on the sponge along its direction of motion
   with more than `F_CUT`, every tie whose two nodes lie within `radius` of the blade's collision
   surface, swept `reach` ahead along its motion, is released (stiffness set to 0). The two freed
   nodes are nudged 0.8 mm apart, each to its own side, so the blade's wedge pushes each side its
   own way.

| Parameter | Scene 05 (cut) | Scene 06 (dissect) |
|---|---|---|
| `F_CUT` (cutting resistance) | 0.8 N | 0.15 N (peeling the layer interface) |
| `radius` | 2.5 mm | 3 mm |
| `reach` | 4 mm | 4 mm |

Nothing is removed, so there is no kerf: the faces part where the blade holds them apart and close
again behind it. Ties sit one mesh cell apart (4.7 mm along y, 3 mm in z in scene 05), so the cut
advances in steps: the blade's force builds up (to ~3.4 N in scene 05, well above `F_CUT`) until
the next column of ties is within reach, then drops. A finer mesh near the cut plane would make it
smoother. **Limits:** the cut can only follow the
pre-built plane; the two cut faces do not collide with each other; `F_CUT` is a guess to calibrate.

(An older element-deletion cut, a 3 mm column of tetrahedra removed ahead of the blade, is still in
`rig_tools.createScene(cut_x=...)` without `split`; it is not used by the scenes.)

## Checks built into the runs

- **Force balance** (`scripts/check_balance.py`, run by `run.sh` for scenes 04–06): the vertical
  force all tools apply to the sponge must equal the sum of the eight cells minus the sponge weight.
  Typical median error ~1 µN; the worst single steps (15–25 mN) are where a motion starts or stops,
  because the sponge then has some acceleration.
- **Moment balance** (same script): about the horizontal axes, cells + grasp springs + tool wrenches
  + weight sum to zero to within ~2–6 % of the tool moments. The remainder scales with deformation;
  it is most likely the corotational FEM's internal torque imbalance at large local rotations, not
  the sensors (it does not change with friction, contact distance, stiffness ×10 or the corotation
  variant).
- **At rest** the cells sum to the sponge weight (0.038 N).

## Speed

Each 10 ms step of the tool scenes takes ~0.7–1 s (dominated by the LDL factorisation and the
constraint solve), so they run ~75–100× slower than real time. Ways to go faster (each needs
checking against the balance and a reference run with `scripts/compare_runs.py`): the `"springs"`
cell model (~2.2×), a coarser mesh (`rig.RES`), and `AsyncSparseLDLSolver` (much faster per step,
but it diverged when the grasp springs switched on; worth retrying).
